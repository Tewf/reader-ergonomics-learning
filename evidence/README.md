# Evidence

Four literature reviews, one per kind of study document, each in the steps reader-ergonomics uses for its own
[evidence](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/tree/d408349e4bc0f8e9ac7672645bd2aa393e5e6704/evidence): frame,
review, state of the art, position, the case against, baseline. They are under way and not merged yet.

## Contents

| Review | Question | State |
|---|---|---|
| practice-questions | how the questions inside study material are written, placed and answered | under way |
| flashcards | how prompts for spaced review are written and kept | under way |
| exercise-sets | how a set of exercises is ordered and supported, from worked example to problem | under way |
| instructional-text | how an explanatory text is built around its content: objectives, terms, summaries, boxes | under way |

Every work cited will be listed once in the [bibliography](bibliography.md).

## Method

Each review is a **multivocal literature review**: research and **grey literature** (standards, publishers' and
open textbook guides, practitioner books, tools, question-and-answer sites) in one review, built to a written protocol
after Garousi 2019. The rules the four share are those of the reviews of reader-ergonomics-topic, linked rather than
repeated: [its method](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-topic/blob/1220def7863b5e977a3edd1e30bd168fff1523a5/evidence/README.md#method).

One rule differs here. Education research is indexed first in **ERIC**, the database of the US Institute of Education
Sciences, so every formal string is also counted there, by phrase, through its public interface.

## What these reviews do not repeat

Whether a technique works is reviewed in the standard
([decision 0001](../decisions/0001-take-the-document-not-the-technique.md)). These reviews link it, with one line of
context, and add how the document delivers it.
