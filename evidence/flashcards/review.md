# Review

This file reads the literature on how flashcards are written, made, used and kept, strand by strand.
Whether retrieval and spacing work is settled in the [core review](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/d17ee8e9bc07e0bd06e8ec7734cecd570b4e3b37/evidence/learning-science/review.md) and is not repeated.
"(secondary)" marks a number read in an abstract or a summary. Grey scores are in [sources](sources.md), and a source under 10 of 20 is "example only".

## 0. Existing reviews

- Nakata 2011 scored nine flashcard programs on 17 criteria from paired-associate research; most do little to raise retrieval effort, and no shared design guideline exists (secondary). The author's handbook chapter was not opened (Nakata 2019b).
- Webb 2020 pooled 100 effects from 22 vocabulary studies of flashcards, lists, writing and fill-in-the-blanks: delayed meaning-recall gains averaged 39.4%, and direction of learning moderated gains (secondary).
- Versteeg 2020 (scoping, 120 articles) finds spaced learning in health professions poorly defined (secondary).
- Xu 2024c (systematic) finds flashcards popular and correlated with grades; Öcel 2025 (25 studies) finds Quizlet studies positive (secondary).
- Nguyen 2021 and Teymouri 2024 are narrative reviews of Anki and digital flashcards; Kornell 2007, Bjork 2013 and Kang 2016 review study decisions and spacing (secondary).
- Lei 2022 synthesised 32 word-card studies by effect-size plots, without pooling: more showed larger effects for ready-made than self-made cards, and for paper than digital (secondary); no review found compares card formulations.

## 1. Seminal work

- Wozniak 1999 gives twenty rules: understand first, minimum information, cloze, imagery, avoid sets and enumerations, combat interference, optimise wording, context cues, sources and dates.
- It cites no study; its numbers are the author's records, such as one item claimed to save 25-fold.
- Slamecka 1978 named the **generation effect**, memory for what the learner produces over what is read (described through Bertsch 2007).
- Watkins 1975 explained proactive inhibition as **cue overload**: a cue shared by many items retrieves each less well (title only; Nairne 2002 develops the principle).

## 2. Card formulation (Q1)

- Practitioner rules converge: one idea per prompt, one correct answer, no yes or no prompts, no lists, context named (Matuschak 2020; Nielsen 2018; Bjornstad 2020).
- Matuschak 2020 adds that prompts must be effortful and tractable, and says research gives no exact guidance.
- The Anki manual says to understand before memorising and to split cards that hold too much (Anki 2026a; Anki 2026c).
- Direction: first language then second was more versatile when both production and comprehension were needed (Griffin 1996, secondary).
- Pictures: Swahili words were not learned better from pictures than from translations until overconfidence was removed (Carpenter 2012, secondary).
- Pictures on flashcards helped concrete words, and simple pictures hindered abstract ones (Ide 2026, secondary).
- Card elements: word-formation notes went with vocabulary growth, in a model explaining 15.8% of variance (Reynolds 2020, secondary).
- Cloze form: fill-in-the-blank sentences and flashcards gave no overall difference at two weeks, 150 learners (Kim 2023, secondary).
- No comparison of cloze and question-and-answer cards inside a spaced program was found; a Q/A answer reports the same gap (LLSE 2017b).
- Cue strength: a vague hint leaves room for retrieval and an obvious one turns recall into recognition, by a reading of the literature (PNSE 2015).

## 3. Who makes the cards (Q2)

- Pan 2023b, six experiments, 48-hour test: learner-made beat premade cards, d = 0.45 for memory and 0.29 for application (secondary).
- Generating beats reading by about 0.40 over 86 studies (Bertsch 2007, secondary); the size depends on how constrained generation is (McCurdy 2020, secondary).
- Text generation beat reading, g = 0.41, not explained by time on task (Schindler 2023, secondary); learners choose cues that suit their own memory better than observers do (Tullis 2022, secondary).
- Against: premade word cards may be better (Wilkinson 2020, secondary), ready-made cards gave more recall than self-made ones (Sage 2019, secondary), and the word-card synthesis leans the same way (Lei 2022, secondary).
- Deng 2015 (full text, 72 of 111 students): Anki cards, which the authors class as user-made, predicted licensing scores, about 1 point per 1,700 cards; commercial premade cards did not (p = 0.763).
- Deng 2015 is a retrospective survey, and its authors ask for a randomised trial before a causal claim.
- Lu 2021 (full text, 201 respondents): at least 80% used premade decks and 57.4% made cards; users scored higher, d = 0.38.
- Teacher-made cards: one third of nursing students used them, and users scored higher, d = 0.42 (Ingebrigtsen 2025, secondary).
- More teacher-made cards reviewed went with higher physiology grades (Santos-Ferreira 2024, secondary); a class that crowdsourced 16,000 questions outscored the class before (Bow 2013, secondary).
- Premade psychiatry cards were used by 56% and did not predict exam scores (Sun 2021b, secondary).
- Practitioners favour own decks for complex subjects, shared decks as a supplement (Anki 2026a; Nielsen 2018).

## 4. How learners use cards (Q3)

- Dropping cards had small but consistently negative effects on learning, in four experiments (Kornell 2008, secondary).
- Learners remove an item once they can recall it, which leads to poor retention (Karpicke 2009, secondary).
- Dropping after one correct recall saved trials and lowered final scores (Pyc 2011, secondary).
- One large stack beat four small ones, which mass the reviews; spacing won for 90% of learners, and 72% believed the opposite (Kornell 2009, secondary).
- Learners self-test to diagnose, not to learn (Kornell 2009b, secondary).
- Of 374 students, most understood practising to a higher criterion but not longer lags (Wissman 2012, secondary).
- Of 901 students, digital flashcard use only partly followed the evidence (Zung 2022, secondary).
- About 70% of 415 students used flashcards, and use on all three exams went with higher scores (Golding 2012, secondary).
- With a partner, learners dropped fewer cards; alone, their global judgments ran about 20% above their test (Imundo 2025, secondary).
- Required Anki use went with better grades, and with reluctance and little enjoyment (Seibert Hanson 2020, secondary).
- Anki marks a card a leech after 8 lapses and suspends it; its manual's first remedy is to rewrite the card (Anki 2026c).

## 5. Granularity, interference, understanding (Q4)

- No study of the minimum information principle was found under that or a related name (protocol, F7 and F8).
- With spacing equal, studying in small or large sets differed little; spacing mattered more (Nakata 2016, secondary).
- Five or seven retrievals beat one or three, but per minute one retrieval gained most (Nakata 2017, secondary).
- Semantically clustered words were harder to learn (Tinkham 1993; Tinkham 1997, secondary).
- Related words raised confusion errors without lowering scores (Nakata 2019, secondary); words whose referents look alike interfered, semantic relation alone did not (Ishii 2015, secondary).
- Nation 2000 advises against teaching lexical sets together (opening read in a quotation, LLSE 2018b).
- Conceptual cards beat detailed cards for less able learners only, and flashcards overall did not beat free study (Lin 2018, secondary).
- Cards written for comprehension and application raised exam scores in three studies of 73, 62 and 434 students (Senzaki 2017, secondary).
- Nielsen 2019 turns one proof into dozens of cards over a few hours, by his own account.

## 6. Text with embedded prompts (Q5)

- Quantum Country embeds 112 questions in an essay of about four hours' reading (Matuschak 2019).
- Six months after release, 195 users had shown a month of retention on at least 80% of the cards.
- After about 95 minutes of review, demonstrated retention rose from about 2 to about 54 days per card.
- Informal test: regular review moved accuracy from 89% to 96% (16 users), a two-week delay from 91% to 87% (25 users); no essay-without-prompts control.
- "Mnemonic medium" returned no record in ERIC or arXiv; the nearest formal work, adjunct questions, is held by the core.

## 7. Generated cards (Q7)

- Model-made Anki cards held 1 error per 21 cards (4.8%); users' exam scores did not differ, and use was self-selected (Khurana 2025, preprint, secondary).
- Generated contextual cards in Anki against usual teaching gave d = 2.48 in 60 learners, cards and scheduler confounded (Karrar 2026, secondary).
- Experts judged generated MOOC cards accurate (Bachiri 2025, secondary); generated keyword cues were tested against hand-made ones (Lee 2023, abstract only).
- Students' stated and observed preferences for generated mnemonics disagreed, over 2,684 preferences from 45 students (Balepur 2024, secondary).
- Six students wanted generated cards editable and transparent (Li 2026, secondary).
- No randomised comparison of generated and written cards on delayed recall was found.

## 8. Practice and adoption (Q6)

- Repository stars: Anki 32,379, AnkiDroid 12,116, the FSRS add-on 4,119, Orbit 1,838 (GitHub 2026h, read 2026-10-29).
- In 2020, 68.3% of 560 students from 102 US medical schools used Anki (Halperin 2024, self-selected, secondary).
- Daily Anki users were 56% of 165 at one school (Wothe 2023, secondary), and about 70% of 60 used it at another (Harris 2022, secondary).
- Anki users scored higher in one more single-school cohort (Gilbert 2023); in another, heavier users scored higher on average, not significantly (Levy 2023, 45 students) (both secondary).
- Of 57 Ottawa respondents, 98.2% used unofficial resources, student-made Anki decks among the most common (Makus 2023, secondary).
- Quizlet's own page refused the request; an uncited figure gives 60 million monthly users in 2021 (Wikipedia 2026e, example only).
- Guides add cost rules: memorise what would cost over 5 or 10 minutes to look up (Branwen 2019; Nielsen 2018).

### Q/A sites

Language Learning, Psychology and Neuroscience, Mathematics Educators and Academia Stack Exchange (read 2026-10-29): 19 threads, 2 under 10 of 20; opinion, no data.

- Hiding the word trains production, hiding the meaning trains comprehension; study both as goals require (LLSE 2016a).
- Split a word with several meanings, or give it a short sentence (LLSE 2018a); put grammar into cloze sentences (LLSE 2016h).
- Flashcards give a shallow, context-free meaning; combine them with reading (LLSE 2016b).
- Leeches come from confusable pairs and misunderstanding; separate the pair in time (LLSE 2016c; LLSE 2016e).
- An old card costs little per review, yet 20,000 cards at 10 seconds is about 55.5 hours over a few years (LLSE 2016d); daily use and backlogs cost most (LLSE 2017a).
- Premade parts that help recognise a word are fine; parts that help use it should be personal (LLSE 2016f).
- Mathematics is compressed by problems and mental models more than by prompts (MESE 2014; MESE 2024).
- Universities leave memorisation to students (AcSE 2016; AcSE 2024); sharing a set is fair, questions first (AcSE 2023, example only).
- Examples only: a new card appears before it is learned, so learn it first (LLSE 2016g); a reversed sibling only shortens intervals (PNSE 2016).
