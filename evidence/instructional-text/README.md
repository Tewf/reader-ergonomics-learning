# Instructional text: the explanatory study text and its apparatus

Question: which parts a study text needs around its explanation, and what the evidence says about each. The problem in one sentence is in the [frame](frame.md).

This is a multivocal literature review (Garousi 2019) by one reviewer, searched on 2026-10-29.

- It covers published research, public practice and Q/A sites.
- It includes 69 new formal sources and scores 27 grey sources out of 20 (13 from the web and books, 14 Q/A threads). Also 8 fall below the threshold of 10 and are used as examples only.
- Whether a technique works (objectives, skeletal notes, signalling, worked examples) stays in the core and is linked.
- A number read only in an abstract is marked "(abstract)" in review.md and "(secondary)" in the other files.

## Claims

| Claim | Grade | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A text that states and refutes a misconception beats other conditions, mostly expository texts (g = 0.41, 44 comparisons; against expository text alone g = 0.36, 30 comparisons); a second pool of 294 effects agrees | measured (two meta-analyses; the first read in full, the second secondary) | review.md 0, 5 |
| Whether the refutation effect lasts and holds across topics is unsettled; most tests are immediate and on one topic | supported with caveat (critical review) | review.md 0; against.md |
| Guided notes raise college achievement, d = 0.55; 0.78 against students' own notes, 0.27 against full instructor notes | measured (meta-analysis, 12 studies, read) | review.md 6 |
| Learners value bold terms, glossaries, summaries and self-tests most | measured, descriptive (surveys, secondary) | review.md 2 |
| Reported use and felt helpfulness of textbook aids do not predict exam scores | measured, descriptive (two correlational studies, secondary) | review.md 2; against.md |
| Specific objectives direct reading time and recall to what they name | measured (experiments, abstracts); the trade-off is in the core (Klauer 1984) | review.md 3 |
| Aligning objectives, teaching and tests raises learning | supported with caveat (before-and-after and correlational only; no controlled trial found) | review.md 3; against.md |
| An end summary beat an opening one on the same page (an opening one on a separate page did as well); a short illustrated summary can match the full text | supported with caveat (small, old experiments) | review.md 4 |
| Glossaries and in-text glosses are the weakest gloss forms for learning words | measured, second language only (meta-regression, secondary) | review.md 4 |
| Printed explanations added to worked solutions give little; withholding them can teach concepts better | measured (meta-analysis of 21 experiments; three experiments; secondary) | review.md 7 |
| Making a crib sheet does not teach, and expecting to use one breeds dependency | supported with caveat (small within-student experiments with no group that did not build a sheet, secondary) | review.md 6; against.md |
| Provided study guides help, or hurt, depending on the study | measured, contested | review.md 6; against.md |
| Open textbooks teach as much as commercial ones (g = 0.01, k = 22) | measured (meta-analysis, read) | review.md 0 |
| Objectives first, key points or summary last, checks with answers: the shared template of OpenStax, the Carpentries and a course rubric | convention, adopted (descriptive comparison) | state-of-the-art.md |
| Backward design and course design rubrics improve courses | convention, untested (one non-significant pilot) | review.md 3, 8 |

## Named baseline

- The open-textbook section with its usual apparatus: objectives, bold terms defined in context, section summary, concept check with a key (OpenStax 2026b).
- For a single lesson: the Carpentries episode, questions and objectives, challenges with solutions, key points (Carpentries 2026a).
- Comparison: same content and time, objective-tagged and transfer items one week later, several topics, effect sizes with intervals (baseline.md).
