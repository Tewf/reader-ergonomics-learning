# State of the art, per sub-question

Two records are kept apart: **most adopted** (what textbooks and lessons do) and **best evidence** (what a study measured). Upper bounds (what has been shown) are kept apart from lower bounds (what has not).

| Sub-question | Most adopted | Best evidence | Measured how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 which aids, used, effective | objectives, bold key terms, summary, checks with a key (OpenStax 2026b) | Gurung 2003; Gurung 2004; Weiten 1999 | student ratings and reported use against exam scores; correlational |
| Q2 objectives | objectives at the start of each section or episode (OpenStax 2026b; Carpentries 2026a) | Klauer 1984 (core); Britton 1985 | meta-analysis of objectives with texts; reading-time experiment |
| Q2 alignment | rubric review of alignment (Quality Matters 2026b); backward design in lesson training (Carpentries 2026b) | none controlled; Larkin 2013, Lynch 2020 | one programme before and after; a pilot of two against two courses, not significant |
| Q3 summaries | a section summary at the end (OpenStax 2026b); key points at the end of an episode (Carpentries 2026a) | Hartley 1979; Cook 1981; Mayer 1996; van der Meij 2012 | small experiments on recall and transfer |
| Q3 glossaries | bold terms defined in context, plus a glossary | Yanagisawa 2020 (second-language only) | meta-regression of 42 studies on word learning |
| Q4 refutation | misconception notes in teaching guides; not counted | Schroeder 2022; Danielson 2025 | two meta-analyses, between-subjects experiments |
| Q5 guided notes | instructor handouts; not counted | Larwin 2013 | meta-analysis, 12 college studies |
| Q5 study guides | publisher study guides and instructor concept lists; not counted | Dickson 2005b; Cushen 2019 | classroom experiments, opposite results |
| Q5 crib sheets | allowed in some exams (Academia 2018b) | Dickson 2005 (core); Dickson 2008; Funk 2011 | classroom experiments with surprise tests |
| Q7 explained solutions | student and instructor solutions manuals (OpenStax 2026b) | Wittwer 2010 | meta-analysis of 21 experiments |

## What the templates share

| Part | OpenStax section (OpenStax 2026b) | Carpentries episode (Carpentries 2026a) | Rubric (Quality Matters 2026b) |
|---|---|---|---|
| objectives at the start | yes, measurable by its own account | yes, with questions, required | objectives (standards 2.1, 2.2) head the alignment set |
| key terms | bold, defined in context | no rule found | no rule found |
| summary | section summary | key points, required | no rule found |
| checks | concept check before the exercises | challenges, with solutions nested inside | assessment (3.1) in the alignment set |
| answers | key at the back; solutions manuals | inside the challenge | no rule found |
| boxes | named features (profiles, curiosities, technology) | callouts, instructor notes | no rule found |

- "no rule found" means the page read did not name one.
- All three put objectives first; only the Carpentries template makes them required by its tooling, and the page read does not say which rules its lesson checker applies.

## Upper bounds (shown)

- Refutation text beats expository text on misconceptions, with no moderator found among 26 (Danielson 2025, secondary).
- Guided notes beat students' own notes in college, d = 0.78, and the instructor's full notes, d = 0.27 (Larwin 2013, read).
- A summary of captions and drawings matched or beat the full text on transfer (Mayer 1996, secondary).
- Numbered in-text summaries raised recall, d = 1.57, in one study of 40 (van der Meij 2012, secondary).
- Glossed reading taught more words than plain reading in a second language (Yanagisawa 2020, secondary).

## Lower bounds (not shown)

- No study found removes one pedagogical aid from a real textbook and measures learning.
- No controlled trial of constructive alignment or of a course design rubric on learning was found.
- No study of an end glossary in a first-language study text was found.
- No study of the revision guide as a genre was found.
- Durability of the refutation effect is not settled: Guzzetti 2000 reports long-term effects, and Zengilowski 2021 finds the field's tests mostly immediate.
- No added benefit of printed explanations in worked solutions over self-explaining was found (Wittwer 2010).
